Four ways to find a signal others miss

  1. Look for what's missing. A benefits page with everything except lunch.
  2. Look at related purchases. Whoever already runs perks makes this kind of decision.
  3. Time it to the budget. Q4 benefits planning, and new tax-free amounts on 1 January.
  4. Use employees' own words as proof. Reviews that say "nowhere to eat".

Scalability: cost and speed

  • Scraping: job-ad actors charge per result, $1–5 per 1,000 ads (LinkedIn Jobs from $1, Indeed $3, StepStone $5 per 1,000).
  • AI: Featherless is a flat subscription with unlimited tokens ($25 a month for 4 parallel requests), so each extra request costs almost nothing. Concurrency is the limit. At about 3 s per extraction, roughly 4,800 ads an hour. That is an estimate; measure it.
  • Letters: the only real variable cost, and only qualified leads get one. The price per letter in Germany is unknown; ask Pingen.
  • Every Apify run reports its real cost in usageTotalUsd. Put cost per qualified lead on the slide.
  • Ask Bella&Bona at lunch what a typical customer is worth. That gives the ROI on our cost per lead.

Data probe, 3 Oct, 10:50

  • Holidu (a Bella&Bona customer): the careers page lists no benefits at all.
  • trivago careers page: HTTP 403, blocked.
  • StepStone ads list full benefits, including "Pluxee Essensgutscheine" (meal vouchers) and "Kantine" (canteen).
  • Conclusion: careers pages are unreliable, job ads are the source.

Open risks

  • Judges may read job ads as "hiring signal". Say it explicitly in the pitch: we read the benefits, not the vacancy.
  • Recruitment-agency ads hide the employer. Filter them out.
  • The same company appears under several name spellings. Normalize names before grouping.
  • Postal advertising needs a way to opt out (Werbewiderspruch). Keep a suppression list.
  • Apify may already run an ideas board for actor requests, which would weaken B. Not checked.
